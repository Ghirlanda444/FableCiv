# Chibilization: proposal for a game that stands on its own

Goal: keep everything players love (a cute, deep, turn-based empire game) while making the game clearly its
own thing next to the Civilization series. Legal exposure comes from expression (names, coined terms, copied
choices), not from mechanics, so the plan is: own vocabulary, own systems where the series has a signature
one, and a roster and uniques that nobody else has.

## 1. The name

A subtitle does not reduce trademark similarity: in a dispute the dominant element ("Chibilization" vs
"Civilization") is what gets compared, and "The Cute Path of History" would be treated as descriptive.
Three honest options:

| Option | Risk | Comment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chibilization | high | Strong parody appeal, but a one-letter play on a registered game mark. Needs a trademark search and a lawyer's opinion before any store listing. |
| Chibi Ages | low | Short, ownable, works as a brand and a YouTube channel name. Tagline: "The cute path of history". Recommended. |
| Tiny Empires / Chibi Realms / Pocket Ages | low | Fine fallbacks if "Chibi Ages" is taken in the stores. |

Recommendation: register **Chibi Ages** (or the first free one) and keep "The cute path of history" as the tagline.
The logo art can be regenerated with the new word for about 10 cents.

## 2. Vocabulary already changed (done in this pass)

- Chibipedia instead of Civilopedia; Sparks and Insights instead of Eurekas and Inspirations.
- Every technology (104) and civic (60) has a new name: Ceramics, Herding, Bowcraft, Star Charts, Saddlery,
  Black Powder, Movable Type, Steam Engines, Thinking Machines… and First Laws, Caravans, Statecraft, Vassalage,
  Sellswords, Age of Reason, Home Front, Moonshot, Viral Culture…
- Every generic unit: Militia, Pathfinder, Sling Hunter, Bowman, Spear Guard, Swordbearer, Horse Raider,
  Stone Thrower, Oarship, Arbalester, Pike Guard, Chevalier, Musketeer, Great Cannon, Carrack, Field Gun,
  Dragoon, Fusilier, Monitor, Howitzer, Regulars, Gunners, Dreadnought, Land Cruiser, Mechanized Troops,
  Rocket Battery, Torpedo Ship, Attack Sub, Heavy Armor, Commandos, Heavy Bomber, Jet Interceptor,
  Preacher, Evangelist, Faith Warden, Pioneers.
- Difficulty levels: Newcomer, Villager, Captain, Duke, Monarch, Sovereign, Legend, Myth.
- All 42 quotes from authors still under copyright replaced with public-domain lines.
- Unique units keep their historical names (Legion, Samurai, Eagle Warrior, Janissary…). That is history, not
  Firaxis. The Aztec unique is already the Eagle Warrior.

## 3. Uniques: two per civilization, mixed freely

Today every civilization has one unique unit and one unique building. Proposal: every civilization gets
**two uniques drawn from four kinds**, so the roster stops looking like a template and each civ plays differently.

| Kind | What it is | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unique unit | replaces a generic unit | Legion, Samurai, Winged Hussar |
| Unique building | replaces a building | Roman Bath (aqueduct), Madrasa (university) |
| Unique improvement (new) | a tile improvement only that civ's citizens build | Inca Terrace (hill farm), Dutch Polder (coast farm), Egyptian Sphinx (culture on desert), Khmer Baray (flood tank), Mongol Ordu (pasture that trains cavalry) |
| Unique town specialization (new, ours) | a 6th specialization only that civ can pick | Phoenician "Trade Colony" town, Zulu "Ikhanda" warrior town, Persian "Satrapy" gold town |

Balance rule: each unique is worth roughly one "power point" (a +2 yield building, a +4 strength unit,
a +1/+1 improvement). Warlike civs take unit + unit, builders take building + improvement, traders take
improvement + town specialization. Concrete first batch:

- Rome: Legion + Roman Bath (aqueduct, +2 happiness).
- Zulu: Impi + Ikhanda town (military towns train units at half price).
- Mongolia: Keshig + Ordu improvement (pastures give +1 production and cavalry +1 XP).
- Inca: Terrace improvement + Machu Picchu-style "Sky Road" (mountain tiles walkable) instead of a unit.
- Netherlands: Polder improvement + Sea Beggar.
- Egypt: Sphinx improvement + Maryannu chariot.
- Persia: Immortal + Satrapy town.
- Carthage: Quinquereme + Cothon (harbor: ships 50% cheaper).
- Khmer: Domrey + Baray improvement.
- Phoenicia (new): Bireme + Trade Colony town.

Unique improvements also give the map more visual variety, which matters for a game sold on its looks.

## 5. Free Cities: replacing envoys

Independent cities stay in the game, but the "send tokens to become suzerain" loop goes. Proposal: **Ties**.

- Each free city has a **specialty** (grain, timber, silver, horses, scholars, shrines…) and a **mood**.
- You do not send tokens. Ties grow through **actions**: a Caravan unit running a trade route to them (new
  unit, also gives gold), a military unit garrisoned inside their borders (protection), completing their
  requests (already in the game), sharing a religion, and gifts.
- Ties decay slowly when you ignore them, so relationships need upkeep, not a one-time deposit.
- Four tiers with clear, visible rewards:
  1. Acquaintance: you see their land and can trade.
  2. Partner: their specialty flows to you (a luxury or a strategic resource, or +2 of a yield).
  3. Patron: their unique unit becomes buildable in your capital; open borders; they refuse others' caravans
     in wartime.
  4. Kin: they fight alongside you and, after 20 turns as Kin and sharing a border, you can propose a
     **Union**: they join your empire peacefully as a Town that keeps its specialty as a permanent bonus.
- Rivalry: a civilization that attacks a free city loses all Ties with every other free city for 30 turns.

Why it is better for us: it uses units on the map instead of an abstract counter (more to show in videos),
it rewards attention rather than a gold dump, and it has an ending (Union) that Civ does not have.

## 6. Other improvements, in order of business value

1. **Guided first 30 turns**: an advisor bubble on each new screen (found the capital, choose a tile, research,
   first Spark, first Great Person). Retention on mobile lives or dies here.
2. **Shareable end-of-game card**: one image with the map, score curve, wonders, leader portrait. Free
   marketing on every finished game.
3. **Combat preview with the bonus breakdown** (counters, flanking, walls, Great General) so the new combat
   rules are visible.
4. **Localization**: Italian first, then Spanish, German, French, Portuguese, Japanese. The text is already
   centralized in data files.
5. **Sound effects** (taps, battles, notifications) to go with the music.
6. **Save slots and a turn-by-turn replay** (the replay doubles as timelapse content for the channel).
7. **Victory screens with key art** per victory type.
8. **Difficulty and AI tuning pass** at Duke and above once the new systems settle.
9. Store strategy: free web version as the demo, paid Android/Windows build, later civ packs as add-ons.

## 7. What I would not change

Wonders, resources, terrain, eras, governments and the historical leaders are shared history, not anybody's
property; renaming Stonehenge or Monarchy would only confuse players. Hex tiles, tech trees and one-unit-per-tile
combat are genre conventions used by many games.
