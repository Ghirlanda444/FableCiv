# Divergence (v2) — merge checklist and release note

Branch `v2-divergence` carries the whole Divergence rule set next to the classic rules. Nothing changes for classic players: every v2 path is gated on `g.v2`, classic saves load as before, and the classic test suite (`tests/sim.js`, `tests/e2e.js`, `tests/turnfixes.js`, `tests/uniques.js`, `tests/mastery.js`) passes unchanged.

## How a player picks it
Title → New game → **Rules**: *Classic* (default) or *Divergence (beta)*. The choice is remembered in the device settings. Scenarios use the classic rules.

## What Divergence changes (release note)
- **No research.** Sparks 💡 fire from what the empire does. Seven ages (Pebble, Marble, Turret, Easel, Puffstack, Glowbit, Pixel), each with 32 foundation and 18 branched Sparks; 20 (first age) or 24 foundation Sparks open a Turning Point into the next age, and an age lasts at least 35 turns. Paired Sparks lock their twin for good. Knowledge 📚 can study any Spark of the current age instead of waiting for it: cheap ones 30, foundation 60, branch 90, times 1 + half the age, and each study in the same age makes the next 15% dearer (an accelerator, never a replacement for doing things). The AI studies foundation Sparks of its age first.
- **Insights 🔮** replace civics, governments and policy cards: one-time decisions that become permanent traits. Heritage 🎭 (Culture) piles up: every 8 Heritage a turn adds 1 Influence, and once per age it pays for a **Reform** (120 × (1 + 0.75 × age)) that takes back one decided Insight and picks its other branch (Turning Points excepted).
- **Claims and Influence 🎯.** A settlement starts with its centre and one worked tile and claims one tile per growth; the first three are free, then each costs Influence and needs a Boundary Marker (a Growth Hall lets claims queue). Uprooters carry population between settlements.
- **Rivers.** A river settlement has an easy start: its river tiles are claimed free of Influence and Boundary Markers (when a claim is blocked only river tiles light up), it grows 20% cheaper until 8 population, the river soaks up 1 Smoke, and orders to units on your own river tiles cost 1 Command however far they stand.
- **Warbands.** Up to three fighters plus a Bandleader share a tile and fight as one exchange (100/60/40%), damage lands on the weakest first, zones of control stop movement next to enemies.
- **New units.** Bandleader (leads a Warband), Uprooters (carry a citizen between settlements) and the Scarecrow Crew: bait that every other empire sees as a full Warband; whoever attacks it loses the rest of the turn and fights 4/6/8 Strength weaker for 10/15/20 turns depending on the crew's bait level (raised by the Tanner's Yard and Patent Office Sparks).
- **Smoke (pollution).** Industry makes Smoke per settlement (Workshop +1, Ironworks +2, Factory +3, Power Plant +4, Railway +1, worked Mines and Quarries +0.5, crowds from the Puffstack Age on), woods and civic works soak it up (woods 0.5 each, Aqueduct 1, Hospital 1, Sewer 2, National Park 3, Sparks). Net Smoke costs 1 Happiness per 3 (at most 4); at 6 the settlement is heavy: farms and pastures lose 1 Food and its Fame is halved. Haze shows on both maps; the AI builds sinks when smoky.
- **Climate shifts.** World warmth follows the ages (warm Turret Age, cold Easel Age) and then the world's Smoke (mild under 12, warm under 30, hot above). A shift is announced five turns ahead and converts at most 3% of the land at biome borders: tundra thaws, snow becomes tundra, or the reverse; a hot world dries plains next to deserts and drowns a few flat unowned shores. Settlements, wonders and tiles with units never change.
- **Richness.** Fewer resource tiles, each Poor/Normal/Rich; a strategic tile supports 1–3 units.
- **Strategic resources and Synthesis.** Four new strategic resources in both rule sets: Copper (Stone Thrower, Arbalester), Rubber (Land Cruiser, Biplane, Heavy Bomber), Aluminum (Jet Interceptor, Rocket Battery, Airport) and Uranium (Attack Sub, Heavy Armor, Nuclear Plant); Oil keeps the Torpedo Ship and Mechanized Troops. Every strategic resource is guaranteed on the map at least twice (or three quarters of the empires). Under Divergence a resource is revealed by the age of its classic technology (Horses, Copper and Iron at once; Niter in the Turret Age; Coal and Rubber in the Puffstack Age; Oil and Aluminum in the Glowbit Age; Uranium in the Pixel Age); before this fix nothing strategic was ever revealed under Divergence. From the Glowbit Age a City with a Research Lab can **synthesise**: 300 Knowledge (60% more each time) buys a lasting +2 supply of any known strategic resource, so late Knowledge is a strategic currency. The AI synthesises what it lacks. Pixel-age Spark pair: Lab-Grown Everything (first synthesis, Synthesis 30% cheaper) against Strategic Reserve (own 4 kinds, +2 Knowledge in the capital per kind).
- **Kinfolk.** Each free city listens to one persuasion that counts double; a Bond (Influence upkeep) replaces the Union.
- **Moods.** Five moods from Miserable to Joyful scale yields and growth; Migration Pull moves families from Miserable neighbours into Joyful empires every ten turns.
- Abilities, wonders and the Great Scientist that spoke of technologies or civics have Divergence wording and equivalents (see docs/V2-PLAN.md). Chibipedia, tips and all 8 languages cover the new rules.
- **Ability synergy pass** (`data/v2/abilities.js`, `AU.V2_SYNERGY`): 35 abilities gained one effect tied to the new rules. Both rule sets (Command, unrest): Rome (orders on own roads cost 1, +1 Command), Persia and Roosevelt and Genghis (+2 Command), Shawnee and Trajan (far units cost 1 less), Pachacuti (roads), Cyrus (captured settlements: no unrest, never revolt), Suleiman and Lincoln (unrest half as long). Divergence only: China (claims 25% cheaper), America (four free claims), Egypt +3 and Hatshepsut +2 Influence per river tile claimed, Celts (woods soak double Smoke), Netherlands (1 Smoke sink per settlement), Germany (buildings half Smoke), Victoria (+1 Gold per Smoke), Russia (tiles never shift with the climate), Mongolia, Napoleon and Shaka (Warbands of four), Zulu and Japan (Warband strikes 100/80/60), Qin (Scarecrow bait +1 level), Meiji (Synthesis 30% cheaper), Babylon (25% cheaper), Peter (+3 supply per Synthesis), Catherine and Maya (two Reforms per age), Greece +15 and Ashoka +10 Influence per Insight, Korea (foundation studies refund 25%), Carthage and Arabia (Bonds half upkeep). Effect keys: `roadOrders`, `farOrderDiscount`, `commandBonus`, `noUnrest`, `unrestMult`, `claimCostMult`, `freeClaims`, `riverClaimInfluence`, `forestSinkMult`, `smokeSink`, `smokeSourceMult`, `smokeGold`, `climateImmune`, `warbandSize`, `warbandWeights`, `scarecrowLevel`, `synthCostMult`, `synthSupplyBonus`, `reformsPerAge`, `insightInfluence`, `studyRefund`, `bondUpkeepMult`. Covered by tests/synergy.js.
- **Three kinds of wonder (both rule sets).** Wonders 🏛️ (Stonehenge, Colosseum, Notre Dame, Forbidden City, Taj Mahal, Eiffel Tower, Statue of Liberty): one in the world, any City. Great Wonders 👑 (Pyramids, Great Library, Oracle, Hanging Gardens, Great Lighthouse, Terracotta Army, Petra, Colossus, Machu Picchu, Chichen Itza, Alhambra, Big Ben, Hubble): one in the world, raised only in the capital (at 150% cost) or in a Town of the matching trade (`home`: mining, farming, trade, fort, urban). A Town raises one Great work at a time; while it does, its Production builds it instead of turning into Gold (`G.enqueue` for Towns, `y.townProduction`, `G.pruneQueue` on a change of trade or an upgrade). National Wonders 🏯: once per empire; Oxford University (new, Urban Center, +8 Knowledge, +1 per University), Royal Library, National University, National Museum (urban), Ironworks (mining), National Treasury, Central Bank (trade), Heroic Epic, Grand Arsenal (fort) rise only in a Town of that trade. The AI specialises one Library town as an Urban Center, leaves a Great Wonder to a Town of its trade when it has one, and Towns raise Great works at pop 4+. Wonder effects for the new rules: Big Ben and Alhambra +1 Command, Notre Dame and Statue of Liberty halve unrest (both rule sets); under Divergence the Pyramids give a free claim, the Great Library cheaper studies, the Oracle Influence per Insight, the Hanging Gardens Influence per river claim, the Great Lighthouse +2 Influence, the Terracotta Army a Scarecrow level, Petra cheaper claims, the Colossus cheaper Bonds, Machu Picchu road orders, Chichen Itza stronger woods, the Forbidden City an extra Reform, Hubble cheaper Synthesis, Stonehenge and the Taj Mahal Heritage per settlement, the Eiffel Tower Fame (`fxV2`, merged in `G.civFx`). Covered by tests/wonders.js.
- **Synergy tone-down** after the first ladder: Rome keeps the road rule but no Command bonus; Persia, Roosevelt and Genghis +1 Command (was +2); Greece +8 and Ashoka +6 Influence per Insight (was 15/10); Egypt +2 and Hatshepsut +1 per river tile (was 3/2); China claims 20% cheaper (was 25%); Celts woods ×1.5 (was ×2); Germany buildings 30% less Smoke (was 50%); Zulu and Japan strike 100/70/50 (was 100/80/60); Meiji Synthesis 20% cheaper (was 30%), Babylon 15% (was 25%); Korea refund 20% (was 25%). The four-fighter Warband is gone: Mongolia's Warband fighters move +1 (`warbandMoves`), Napoleon's Bandleader adds 25% instead of 15% (`commanderMult`), Shaka's Warband fighters heal +5 (`warbandHeal`).
- **Ships strike the shore (both rule sets).** A naval melee unit may attack units and settlements on the land tile beside it (any coast, and along navigable rivers); it stays at sea and can level a settlement's walls to 1 HP but never captures (only soldiers walk in; Warband path too). Keys: `navalRaidBonus` (ships striking the shore), `navalVsSettlements`.
- **Sea peoples, each with a different sea.** Portugal: worked Coast and Ocean tiles +1 Gold. Polynesia: coastal settlements founded with 2 population and two coast tiles claimed (`coastalFoundPop`, `coastalFoundWater`). Indonesia: ships cost 30% less (Gitarja now gives coastal settlements +2 Heritage and +1 Happiness instead). Norway: orders to ships and embarked units cost 1 Command anywhere (`seaOrders`), ships striking the shore +5. Carthage: units bought with Gold cost 25% less (`unitPurchaseMult`, mercenaries). England: ships +6 against settlements, settlements on another continent get one more free claim (`abroadFreeClaims`).
- **Round 2 from the 192-game ladder.** Capture Knowledge is halved under Divergence (Assyria, Alexander, Ashurbanipal: conquests were buying Sparks that bought armies). Zulu strikes 100/65/45. The AI arms against a stronger neighbour it has met: its soldier target is at least 60% of the largest known army (capped at 3 per settlement + 3) and never below settlements + 2, which is what killed Korea, Maya and Russia by turn 90.
- **Fair starts.** Every start's first ring holds at least three 2-Food tiles (poor plains, tundra or desert beside the start turn to grassland) and a food resource lies within two tiles (one is placed when none is near); tests/starts.js. A small settlement (pop ≤ 4, surplus ≤ 1) claims food first. The AI buys the Boundary Marker outright when a claim is blocked and the treasury allows. Traced on Korea seed 32: the capital sat at 2 population for 30 turns with a claim blocked for 20 of them and died at turn 63; now 3 settlements, 18 population and 6 soldiers at turn 60.
- **The hearth guard (Divergence).** A people defending its own land against a wider empire fights harder: units on their own territory and settlement walls get +2 Strength per settlement the attacker holds beyond the defender's, at most +8. It makes the early rush by a warlord neighbour expensive without coalitions or mercy rules; a big empire still wins with siege and numbers, it just pays for it.
- **Divergence is the game.** The Rules chooser is gone: New game always starts Divergence, and every ability, wonder and Great Person description shows its Divergence wording everywhere (`G.abilityDesc` prefers `descV2` unconditionally). The classic engine paths remain for the test suites only.
- **Mouse orders on a computer.** Left button: select, read, cycle the units on a tile, pan by dragging; it never moves a unit. Right button: move or attack with the selected unit; hold it to see the route and release on the target (a plain right-click works too). Touch input is unchanged. The toast no longer intercepts pointer events (it used to swallow a click on the tile under it).
- **Civ ladder** (`tools/civ-ladder.js <games> <turns> <v2> [seed]`, `--summary <log>`): AI-only games with a random civ and leader for the player seat, ranked by score; the summary lists average rank, win share and elimination share per civ/leader so an ability that wins or loses too often stands out.

## Balance of power (both rule sets): Command
Four 300-turn AI games showed one empire swallowing the map. The structural answer is **Command**, an order budget in the spirit of Old World's orders: every turn an empire can order 5 units, plus 1 per settlement, plus command buildings (Palace 3, Barracks 1, Castle 1, Military Academy 2, Telegraph Office 2, Railway Station 1, Airport 2, Grand Arsenal 2), minus bureaucracy beyond eight settlements (1 per 3) and minus 1 per settlement in unrest. A unit's first move or attack of the turn spends 1 Command, 2 when it is more than 5 tiles from any settlement of the empire, 3 beyond 10. A Warband strikes on one order; an undone move refunds it; units out of Command can only fortify, skip or sleep. The AI gives its orders to settlers and units near enemies first and holds the rest. The 🎖️ pill on the top bar shows orders left; the unit card shows each unit's cost. Sparks raise it under Divergence (Big Chief, War College, The Wire).

Around it, the realistic frictions stay: a captured settlement suffers 10+ turns of unrest (half yields, -3 Happiness) and while unrest lasts an unhappy conquest far from its new capital can rise up and return to its living owner; each capture adds war weariness; a truce holds 25 turns (40 after a war that took settlements); an attacker that took two settlements accepts peace; a very large empire is trusted less (-1 attitude a turn from every leader) and its player is warned once. There are no coalitions, no spared last settlements and no war ban: a big empire is beaten by its own reach, not by scripted alliances.

Result over four 300-turn AI games: survivors 3–4 of 5 (from 1–2), and the two largest empires end close in size (17 vs 18, 16 vs 12, 15 vs 19, 10 vs 15 settlements) instead of one empire with 30–38.

## Known gaps (not blocking the merge)
- City Focus slots (spec item) are not built; Town specializations still work under Divergence.
- Great People from Insight payoffs (`spawnNamed` hook) are not built; Great People still come from points.
- No pictures yet for Uprooters, Bandleader, Scarecrow Crew, Cookfire, Kiln, Playfield, Boundary Marker and Growth Hall (the game draws its fallback glyphs). Generating them is a paid art run: the owner decides.
- The `cookfire`, `kiln` and `playfield` improvements named by Era 1 Sparks do not exist as improvements yet (the Sparks still give their yields).

## Merge steps
1. Merge the pull request of `v2-divergence` into `main` (a merge commit; the branch history is linear on top of main).
2. The Pages deploy, the Android APK and the Windows build run from `main` as usual; players get the "New version ready" banner.
3. Smoke-check on the phone: New game → Rules: Divergence → found the capital → the 🔬 box shows `0/32 💡`.

## Roll back
Revert the merge commit on `main` (`git revert -m 1 <merge sha>`), or set Rules to Classic: every v2 system is inert for classic games, so a rollback is only needed if the classic path regresses.
